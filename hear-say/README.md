## A public record of lived experiences with institutions

Hear Say exists to give people a place to document and preserve their experiences with the Family Court and other public institutions, particularly where they believe they were not heard, treated fairly, or given meaningful access to justice.

The live publication has since expanded beyond the original MVP into politics, old cases, source-backed public-interest reporting, lived experience, public participation, right of reply and visible corrections. The project-memory file above is authoritative for the current direction.

### What this platform is for

- Personal experiences and first-person accounts
- Documented timelines of events
- Publicly available decisions and records
- Evidence that contributors are legally able to share
- Patterns that emerge across multiple experiences
- Information about routes for complaints, review, and accountability
- Source-backed public-interest explainers
- Clearly labelled public discussion, corrections and right of reply

### What Hear Say is not

Hear Say is not a court, tribunal, investigation agency, or substitute for legal advice. A person's account is identified as an account unless independently verified. Allegations should be clearly distinguished from established facts.

### Contributor safety

Do not publish children's identifying information, private addresses, contact details, medical information, passwords, restricted court material or confidential documents unless there is a clear lawful basis and genuine public-interest reason to do so. Private Gmail and legal material is not a publication feed.

### Editorial principle

The goal is not to manufacture a conclusion. The goal is to create a careful, searchable and readable public record so that experiences can be compared, evidence can be examined, legitimate disagreement can be recorded and important concerns are harder to lose.

### Current editorial standard

Major stories should go beyond a headline and a source card. Where the evidence permits, they should show what happened, what reliable sources establish, what remains disputed or unknown, the strongest counter-position, why the issue matters, the evidence/reply status, and where the reader should go next.

## Core rule

Never invent evidence, readership, comments, engagement or certainty. Build credibility by showing the reader exactly where certainty ends.
