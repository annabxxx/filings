# Hear Say

## A public record of lived experiences with institutions

Hear Say exists to give people a place to document and preserve their experiences with the Family Court and other public institutions, particularly where they believe they were not heard, treated fairly, or given meaningful access to justice.

### What this platform is for

- Personal experiences and first-person accounts
- Documented timelines of events
- Publicly available decisions and records
- Evidence that contributors are legally able to share
- Patterns that emerge across multiple experiences
- Information about routes for complaints, review, and accountability

### What Hear Say is not

Hear Say is not a court, tribunal, investigation agency, or substitute for legal advice. A person's account is identified as an account unless independently verified. Allegations should be clearly distinguished from established facts.

### Contributor safety

Do not publish children's identifying information, private addresses, contact details, medical information, passwords, or confidential documents unless there is a clear lawful basis to do so. Contributors should redact information that could unnecessarily identify vulnerable people.

### Editorial principle

The goal is not to manufacture a conclusion. The goal is to create a careful, searchable public record so that experiences can be compared, evidence can be examined, and legitimate concerns are harder to lose.

## Proposed sections

1. Stories — first-person accounts
2. Timelines — structured chronology
3. Evidence — documents and source material that can lawfully be published
4. Patterns — recurring issues supported by multiple records
5. Accountability — complaints, responses, reviews and outcomes
6. Resources — official information for people trying to navigate the system

## First milestone

Launch a simple public front page and submission form, then build the searchable evidence and timeline system behind it.
