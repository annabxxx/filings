Half of family-violence victims go without help

A national statistic makes unmet support visible — but it still has to be read carefully.

HEAR SAY editorial desk • Updated 24 August 2026

1 IN 2The Ministry’s 2025 NZCVS key stories say half of family-violence victims go without help.

The 2025 New Zealand Crime and Victims Survey does two things at once: it records a fall in the number of adults experiencing family offences, while also reporting that the number of incidents increased. Its key-stories summary highlights another finding — half of family-violence victims go without help.

Why the statistic matters

A person’s absence from a service cannot safely be treated as evidence that support was unnecessary. Unmet need can sit outside police, health, legal and social-service records precisely because the person did not, or could not, reach those systems.

What the survey does not prove

The national statistic does not establish why any particular person did not seek help, whether a particular agency failed them, or whether every non-user wanted formal intervention. Those are separate factual questions.

The question worth asking

If support systems are evaluated only by the people who successfully reach them, what happens to the people missing from the denominator? HEAR SAY will keep service-use data separate from assumptions about need.
