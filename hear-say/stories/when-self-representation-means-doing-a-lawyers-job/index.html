Doing a lawyer’s job without a lawyer

The right to represent yourself is real. So is the procedural load the Ministry says you must carry.

HEAR SAY editorial desk • Updated 24 August 2026

THE JOB LISTResearch the law • prepare and file • manage procedure • present the case • question witnesses

The Ministry of Justice says a person may represent themselves in court. But its own guidance also makes clear that self-representation is not simply turning up and telling a judge what happened.

What the official guidance requires

For Family Court matters, the Ministry says a self-represented person is expected to correctly prepare and file documents, arrange service where required, present the case to the judicial officer and cross-examine witnesses. Its broader guidance adds legal research, procedural management, compliance with directions, preparation of evidence, submissions and authorities.

The legal right to appear without a lawyer does not remove the lawyer-sized workload.

What that does — and does not — prove

The guidance proves that the procedural burden exists. It does not prove that every self-represented party is treated unfairly, that representation guarantees success, or that every difficult case requires a lawyer. The Ministry itself says representation may be a valid option for people confident in researching, preparing and managing their case.

The access-to-justice question

The sharper question is practical: when one party has professional representation and the other must learn procedure while also living through the dispute, what safeguards make participation genuinely effective? That is a question about process design, not an assumption about any individual judge or outcome.

What HEAR SAY will track

  • What tasks courts expect from self-represented people.
  • What assistance is actually available.
  • Where procedure creates avoidable barriers.
  • What evidence shows those barriers change outcomes, rather than merely making litigation unpleasant.

Reading rule

Official guidance is evidence of the tasks expected. It is not proof of unfairness in a particular proceeding.
