PUBLIC RECORD • LEGAL NEEDS SURVEY

1 IN 3.
THAT IS NOT A SMALL EDGE CASE.

The Ministry of Justice's nationwide Legal Needs Survey found that, in the preceding year, a third of the population experienced an issue that could potentially be resolved through legal action.

KEY FINDINGS
1 IN 3experienced a potentially legal issue
10%experienced 77% of recorded issues
8%of issues prompted no action at all

These are survey findings, not findings about any individual case.

WHAT THE SURVEY ACTUALLY SHOWS

The burden is concentrated.

A headline percentage matters less when you can see who is carrying the repeated problems.

CONCENTRATION77%

Ten percent experienced most of the issues.

The Ministry reports that 10 percent of the population experienced 77 percent of the issues recorded in the survey.

GROUPSHIGHER RISK

Some groups were more likely to encounter legal issues.

The Ministry identifies disabled people, LGBT+ people, single parents, Māori and people with low savings among the groups most likely to encounter legal issues.

INACTION8%

Sometimes people did nothing because action felt pointless or too costly in other ways.

For eight percent of issues, respondents did nothing at all. Common reasons included believing action would make no difference, take too long or be too stressful.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVE

A national survey is context, not a verdict on an individual dispute.

01

It does not prove a specific institution acted unlawfully.

That requires the facts, legal framework and evidence of the individual case.

02

It does not prove every legal problem requires a court.

The survey records that many issues were resolved by mutual agreement and that help often assisted resolution.

03

It does show why “just use the system” can be an incomplete answer.

Legal need is common, repeated problems are concentrated, and stress, delay and perceived futility can affect whether people act.

HEAR SAY QUESTION

Where does formal access fail in practice?

Money, procedure, representation, delay, information and stress are different problems. Good reporting should identify which barrier is actually operating rather than collapsing them into one slogan.
