# UNHEARD AOTEAROA
## A documented access-to-justice chronology from New Zealand

> **Public-interest version.** This page deliberately omits names of children, Family Court case numbers, home addresses, detailed medical records, and other identifying material that may be restricted from publication. It distinguishes documented facts from allegations and unresolved questions.

### What this page is about

This is not a claim that every public official acted unlawfully, nor a claim of conspiracy.

It is a request for independent scrutiny of a documented sequence in which a New Zealand woman repeatedly reported a head-injury history, medical difficulty participating in court, lack of legal representation, and escalating risks to her housing and family life — while different institutions dealt with only narrow parts of the problem.

### The core documented sequence

**Early 2025 — medical warnings.**  
Medical letters recorded significant anxiety and distress associated with multiple legal proceedings. One letter stated that in-person attendance would be highly detrimental and that the patient would not be able to present evidence effectively. A later letter recommended telephone or video participation rather than physical attendance.

**June–July 2025 — requests for legal representation and alternative participation.**  
The court administration confirmed that medical material was on the file and that a Judge had read a medical certificate before making directions. Written memoranda stated that the applicant required legal representation and was “not by choice” without counsel. Requests were also made for remote participation.

**1 July 2025 — proceeding continued without counsel.**  
The proceeding continued without the applicant present. Her application was dismissed and significant filing restrictions were imposed.

**Later 2025 — formal-proof consequences.**  
A later judgment expressly recorded that the other party’s evidence was examined while the applicant’s “evidence and other voluminous material” was put to one side. The same judgment also referred to the need for further reliable information to reduce the risk of injustice.

**26 January 2026 — arrest / removal and injury evidence.**  
Police arrested and transported the woman in connection with a court matter. She alleges excessive force. Independent medical examination shortly afterwards documented multiple bruises and contusions and a neck laceration. The available oversight record says custody footage exists, but that footage does not cover what occurred inside the home before custody.

**February–March 2026 — bankruptcy and loss of occupation.**  
The woman was adjudicated bankrupt. The Official Assignee then took the position that her interest in the family home vested in the estate and supported the sale process. An occupation order followed. These are documented legal events; their validity and procedural fairness remain the subject of legal challenge.

### Who had notice

The record shows that multiple institutions had notice of different parts of the problem:

- **Court administration:** medical evidence, requests for counsel, and requests for remote participation.
- **New Zealand Human Rights Commission:** by July 2025, allegations concerning disability/trauma, lack of accommodation, financial dependence, and access to justice had been raised. The Commission said its dispute-resolution jurisdiction did not extend to acts, omissions or judgments of courts and closed the complaint.
- **Independent Police Conduct Authority:** reviewed complaints about the 26 January 2026 arrest, medical certificate and use of force. It relied on Police records and custody footage and took no further action on the court-linked arrest/charge issues.
- **Crown Law:** later confirmed that it held material raising registry and appellate-process concerns, but said it did not escalate those concerns because investigation and supervision of courts and registries fell outside its role.
- **Ombudsman:** was asked to review aspects of the administrative handling and the adequacy of Crown Law’s response.

### The unresolved public-interest question

> **When several public bodies each receive serious information about medical vulnerability, lack of legal representation, court participation, Police force, housing loss and access to justice, who is responsible for seeing the whole picture before the consequences become irreversible?**

### What independent reviewers are being asked to examine

1. Whether medically documented participation barriers were adequately accommodated.
2. Whether lack of legal representation materially affected the ability to participate.
3. Whether court-received material reliably reached the deciding judicial officer.
4. Whether the arrest / force record has been independently and completely tested, including the full custody footage and the missing underlying warrant/minute/timestamp material.
5. Whether institutional fragmentation meant that no body assessed the cumulative risk before housing, financial and family consequences escalated.
6. Whether New Zealand’s domestic remedies provided an effective avenue for urgent protection in this case.

### Evidence status

- **Documented:** supported by a primary medical, court, agency, or registry record.
- **Alleged:** first-hand account not independently established by the document alone.
- **Official position:** the position stated by Police, IPCA, Crown Law, a court, or another agency.
- **Unresolved:** a question requiring the underlying record or an independent legal determination.

### Current international referrals

A focused submission concerning the 26 January 2026 detention has been acknowledged as received by the **UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention**. Separate requests for assistance or assessment have also been sent to specialist international human-rights and medico-legal organisations.

### Why this page is deliberately restrained

The underlying record contains sensitive health information, Family Court material, and information capable of identifying children. New Zealand law restricts publication of identifying Family Court information in cases involving children or vulnerable people. This public-interest version therefore does **not** publish the full evidential record.

Journalists, lawyers, human-rights organisations and independent reviewers can request a controlled evidence index and source documents for lawful confidential review.
